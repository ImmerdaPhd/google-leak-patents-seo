# Stage 10: Snippet & Answer Extraction

### How this works

Once documents are retrieved and ranked, Google must generate the text the user actually sees: the **title** and **snippet** for each result, and for question queries, the **featured snippet** (answer passage). This is not simply truncating the page's meta description. The leak reveals a sophisticated multi-candidate scoring system where multiple snippet and title candidates are generated from different sources (body text, meta tags, ODP/DMOZ, anchor text, headings), scored by ML models (Muppet, SnippetBrain, Radish), and the best candidate is selected based on query relevance, document quality, and neural features.

The system operates at the **passage level**, not the page level - patent US9940367B1 confirms that "passages are scored independently of their parent document." The Ranklab framework scores both title and snippet candidates using dozens of features, and a separate SnippetBrain model can override the traditional scorer's choice.

### Relevant Patents

#### US9940367B1: Scoring candidate answer passages
- **Core concept:** Passages (not pages) are scored independently to answer question queries directly. The best-scoring passage is selected for featured snippet display.
- **Connected leak modules:** PerDocData, QualityNsrNsrData, QualityNsrPQData
- **SEO action:** Structure direct answers: question as heading, a tight 40-60 word answer, then the depth. Passages are scored on their own. The leak's titlematchScore and contentEffort fields confirm passage-level quality is assessed independently.
- **SEO claims:**
  - Question queries are identified as seeking direct answers rather than resource listings
  - Candidate answer passages are extracted from top-ranking resources
  - Each passage is scored independently of its parent document
  - Scoring considers query-passage relevance, passage clarity, and answer completeness
  - The highest-scoring passage is selected for direct answer display (featured snippets)
  - Passage-level ranking operates independently from page-level ranking

#### US11409748B1: Context scoring adjustments for answer passages
- **Core concept:** Candidate answer passages for question queries are re-scored based on document context signals: heading hierarchy depth, passage coverage ratio, distinctive text formatting, preceding questions, and list detection - all query-independent signals that measure the structural context of the answer within the source page.
- **Connected leak modules:** PerDocData, QualityNsrPQData
- **SEO action:** Structure answer content with proper heading hierarchy (H1→H2→H3) - deeper nesting under relevant headings boosts answer selection. Use bold text for key terms near answers. Format step-by-step instructions as HTML lists. Ensure answer passages are appropriately sized relative to their surrounding text block. Include the question before the answer in your content.
- **SEO claims:**
  - A heading vector is determined describing the path from root heading to the heading under which the candidate answer passage is subordinate; deeper heading depth receives a higher boost factor
  - Passage coverage ratio (answer passage characters / total text block characters) determines boost factors - both very low and very high ratios receive different boost adjustments
  - Heading text in the heading vector is scored for relevance to the question query and answer
  - Heading matches between the query terms and heading text boost the context score
  - Distinctive text (e.g., bolded text that is not a header and not in the answer passage) in the surrounding text block boosts the answer score
  - Preceding questions (a question immediately before the answer passage in the document) boost the context score
  - For step-modal queries (How to, How do I), list-based passages receive a list boost factor, with list quality scored by HTML tags, micro formats, and sequential heading patterns
  - The list boost factor can be increased when other feature scores (coverage ratio, distinctive text) are also high

#### US8819006B1: Rich content for query answers

- **Connected leak modules:** PerDocData, RepositoryWebref
- **SEO action:** Use descriptive alt text, captions, and surrounding context for images near answer content on your pages. Images that contextualize both the question and answer topic are more likely to appear in rich answer results. Ensure your images are properly labeled with relevant entity information.
- **SEO claims:**
  - A contextual query is generated combining elements from both the question query and the answer
  - Three sets of rich content items are retrieved: responsive to contextual query, responsive to question alone, and responsive to answer alone
  - First rich content items (in contextual set but NOT in both question and answer sets) meet the context condition and are preferentially selected
  - Second rich content items (in contextual set AND in question or answer set) are demoted relative to first items
  - Demotion of second items is proportional to their ordinal position in the question or answer set
  - Items appearing in both the question and answer sets receive a harsher demotion than items in only one
  - Rich content items are non-textual content (images, video, audio)
  - The selected rich content items are displayed alongside the answer in search results

#### US11836177B2: Providing knowledge panels with search results

Provide high-quality images through structured data and Wikimedia. Multiple disparate sources feeding the knowledge panel means broader web presence improves panel richness.

  - An interactive scroll bar enables viewing additional images and updating the knowledge panel based on scroll direction

#### US10242112B2: Search result filters from resource content
- **Core concept:** Search result filters are automatically generated by extracting keywords from result page content and related queries, then filtering candidates by diversity threshold (filters must produce different result sets) and quality score (based on keyword position in resources like title).

- **SEO action:** Use relevant keywords in prominent positions (title, headings, early paragraphs) of your pages - these are more likely to become search result filters. Include clear, distinctive terms that differentiate your content from competitors. Structure content with clear categorical terms that would produce diverse filter results.
- **SEO claims:**
  - Keywords are extracted from the contents of resources determined responsive to a query
  - Candidate filters are derived from keywords, forming a proper subset of all extracted keywords
  - Related queries (queries for which resources were previously selected by users) contribute to candidate filter selection
  - Query stop terms (high frequency in related queries) are excluded from candidate filters
  - Informational terms (low frequency in related queries) are included as candidate filters

### How this works

### 1. Multi-Candidate Snippet Generation and Scoring

The leak reveals that Google does not simply use your meta description as the snippet. The `SnippetExtraInfo` module's `candidateInfo` field ("Candidates are ordered by their id") shows that **multiple snippet candidates** are generated for each document. The `SnippetExtraInfoSnippetCandidateInfo` module's `id` field reveals the scoring hierarchy: "Muppet candidates: This equals to the candidate's rank by Muppet snippets scorer. Superroot candidates: No specific meaning, this number should be larger than that of Muppet candidates." This means there are at least two scoring systems - **Muppet** (the traditional scorer) and **Superroot/SnippetBrain** (the ML-based scorer) - and the `isMuppetSelectedSnippet` field tracks which one won.

The `SnippetExtraInfoSnippetScoringInfo` module confirms the dual-model architecture: the `brainScore` field is described as "Score generated from snippet brain model. go/snippets-brain" and `brainNg3Score` tracks the n-gram neural score. The `finalScore` ("Final snippet score by chooser") and `rankBySnippetFlow` ("Final rank given by SnippetFlow") show that a final chooser layer arbitrates between the models. The `disableNg3Scoring` flag in `SnippetExtraInfo` ("Indicates that SR side should disable the ng3 scoring and solely rely on the SnippetBrain scoring for selecting the final candidate") reveals that Google can fully delegate snippet selection to the neural model in some cases.

### 2. Passage-Level Answer Extraction (Featured Snippets)

Patent US9940367B1 establishes that "candidate answer passages are extracted from top-ranking resources" and "each passage is scored independently of its parent document." Patent US11409748B1 then details how these passages are **context-scored** based on document structure: "a heading vector is determined describing the path from root heading to the heading under which the candidate answer passage is subordinate; deeper heading depth receives a higher boost factor."

The leak's `QualityPreviewSnippetRadishFeatures` module implements this passage-level scoring. The `passageCoverage` field ("The ratio of overlapping tokens between the radish passage and snippet candidate") directly measures how much of the passage is represented in the snippet. The `passageType` field ("Integer value of indexing::annotations::wa_passages::Passage::Type") classifies what kind of passage it is - and the `answerScore` ("Answer score of the passage for this") field is the direct implementation of US9940367B1's passage scoring. The `similarityScore` ("Similarity score between this [passage and query]") and `navboostQuery` ("Navboost query for this radish signal") show that passage scoring incorporates both semantic similarity and navigation behavior data.

US11409748B1's "passage coverage ratio (answer passage characters / total text block characters)" is implemented in the `QualityPreviewSnippetQualityFeatures` module through fields like `numVisibleTokensScore`, `redundancyScore`, and `sentenceStartScore` - these measure the structural quality of the passage within its surrounding text block. The patent's "distinctive text (e.g., bolded text that is not a header and not in the answer passage)" claim maps to the `QualitySnippetsTruncationSnippetBoldedRange` module, which tracks `begin` and `end` positions of bolded ranges with a `type` field - Google literally tracks which text is bolded on your page and uses it as a context signal for answer extraction.

### 3. Title Selection via Goldmine/Ranklab

The `QualityPreviewRanklabTitle` module (62 attributes) reveals the full complexity of title selection. Google generates multiple title candidates from different sources - the `sourceTitleTag` ("Title candidate's original source information"), `sourceGeneratedTitle`, `sourceOffdomainAnchor`, `sourceOndomainAnchor`, `sourceHeadingTag`, `sourceGeometry`, `sourceTransliteratedTitle`, and `sourceLocalTitle` fields show that titles can come from the `<title>` tag, generated titles, anchor text (both on-domain and off-domain), heading tags, geometric/layout analysis, transliteration, and local business names.

Each candidate is scored through the "Goldmine" system. The `goldmineFinalScore` ("Deprecated: use [goldmineAdjustedScore]") and `goldmineAdjustedScore` ("The score for [the title]") represent the final title quality score. Dozens of boost factors feed into this: `goldmineTitleTagFactor` (how much the `<title>` tag contributes), `goldmineHeadingFactor` (heading tag contribution), `goldmineBodyFactor` (body text), `goldmineAnchorFactor` ("Internal boost feature signals used to compute [the score]"), `goldmineNavboostFactor` (navigation behavior), `goldmineSitenameFactor` (site name), `goldmineOgTitleFactor` (Open Graph title), `goldmineSiteNameMarkupFactor` (site name structured data), `goldmineSalientTermFactor` (salient terms), `goldmineBlockbertFactor` (a BERT-based model), and `goldminePageScore` ("The score for the [page]").

The `queryMatch` field ("The number of (different) terms with a query match. It may include the match with any SQuery node (e.g., synonyms)") and `queryMatchFraction` ("A number of matched query terms divided by the number of all terms in query") show that title selection is **query-dependent** - the same document may show different titles for different queries depending on which candidate best matches the query terms. Patent US10242112B2's claim that "quality scores for candidate filters are based on locations of the keyword in resources - prominent positions (title) receive higher scores" confirms that title-term matching is a first-class ranking signal, not just a display concern.

### 4. SnippetBrain: Neural Snippet Selection

The `QualityPreviewSnippetBrainFeatures` module reveals a dedicated ML model for snippet selection. The `modelScore` field ("The score by SnippetBrain model") is a neural score, and `isSnippetBrainBoldingTriggered` ("Is the bolding triggered") shows that SnippetBrain even controls which terms are bolded in the snippet. The `SnippetExtraInfo` module's `snippetBrainSelectedCandidateIndex` ("Snippet candidate index selected by snippet brain model") and `snippetsbrainModelInfo` field (containing `ng3ModelName`, `snippetsbrainModelName`, `snippetsbrainModelPartition`, and `snippetsbrainTokenizerType`) confirm this is a production ML pipeline with configurable model versions and tokenizers.

This connects to patent US10452978B2 (the Transformer architecture): "the architecture enables bidirectional understanding of text" and "passage-level understanding becomes feasible - the model reads and comprehends text passages." SnippetBrain is the application of Transformer-based understanding to the snippet selection problem - it reads the document passage and the query, and scores how well the passage answers the query, going far beyond n-gram matching.

### 5. Query Term Coverage and Rich Content

The `QualityPreviewSnippetQueryFeatures` module tracks `queryScore`, `radishScore`, `passageembedScore` ("passage embedding score" - a neural embedding-based relevance measure), and `experimentalQueryTitleScore`. The `QualityPreviewSnippetQueryTermCoverageFeatures` module provides `snippetQueryTermCoverage`, `titleQueryTermCoverage`, and `titleSnippetQueryTermCoverage` - measuring what fraction of query terms appear in the title, snippet, or both. These coverage features feed directly into the Ranklab final score.

Patent US8819006B1's rich content system operates here too: when a question query gets an answer, "rich content items (images, video) are selected" by "generating a contextual query combining question+answer terms." The `WWWSnippetResponse` module's `orionEntities` field (entity annotations), `metaTags`, and `keyword` ("Document keywords") fields provide the entity context that the rich content system uses to match images and video to answers. The `chosenBodyTidbits` ("Tidbits chosen from the document body. Consists of repeated [begin, end) half-open ranges in token offsets") field shows exactly which body text ranges were selected for the snippet - Google tracks the precise character positions of snippet text within the source document.

### SEO actions

- **Structure answer content with proper heading hierarchy (H1→H2→H3).** Patent US11409748B1 confirms that "deeper heading depth receives a higher boost factor" for answer passage selection. The `QualityPreviewSnippetRadishFeatures.passageType` and `answerScore` fields implement this. Nest your answer passages under relevant headings - the deeper and more relevant the heading path, the higher the context boost.

- **Format step-by-step instructions as HTML lists.** US11409748B1 states that "for step-modal queries (How to, How do I), list-based passages receive a list boost factor, with list quality scored by HTML tags, micro formats, and sequential heading patterns." Use proper `<ul>`, `<ol>`, and structured data for instructions.

- **Use bold text for key terms near answers.** US11409748B1's "distinctive text (e.g., bolded text that is not a header)" boosts answer scores. The `QualitySnippetsTruncationSnippetBoldedRange` module literally tracks bolded ranges on your page. Bold the important terms in and around your answer passages.

- **Optimize your `<title>` tag but know it's one of many candidates.** The `QualityPreviewRanklabTitle` module shows Google generates title candidates from title tags, headings, anchor text, OG tags, and more. The `goldmineTitleTagFactor` is just one boost factor among dozens. Write a clear, descriptive `<title>`, but also ensure your H1, Open Graph tags, and anchor text are consistent - any of them could become the displayed title.

- **Include the question before the answer in your content.** US11409748B1 states that "preceding questions (a question immediately before the answer passage in the document) boost the context score." Write your content as question→answer pairs: the question as a heading, followed by a tight 40-60 word answer passage, then expanded depth.

- **Use descriptive alt text and captions for images near answer content.** Patent US8819006B1's rich content system selects images that "contextualize both the question and answer." The `WWWSnippetResponse` entity and metadata fields show Google uses structured data to match images to answers. Properly labeled images near answer content are more likely to appear in rich answer results.
